# Midam Clinic — follow-up resources (promised to the 원장, 2026-09-16)

Commitment made: send materials to help the decision. Requested visual, not text-heavy.

**Governing constraint:** everything sent is about *his clinic*, not our product. The
default instinct — a company deck with features and vision — is the least persuasive
thing available. He has 300 unanswered DMs; he does not care what we are.

**Positioning correction (from `deal_risks.md` R4):** do not build this around "we
automate your Instagram DMs". 채널톡 already sells unified Kakao/LINE/Instagram DM and
애프터닥 owns clinic CRM messaging. Build it around the **qualification layer** — reply
in-language, qualify against the clinic's own 비급여 price list, travel window, visa,
timezone-aware first response, deposit capture against no-shows, and a *qualified,
priced, date-bounded* patient handed to staff.

## Assets, in order of persuasive weight

### 1. DM mockups — phone frames, Traditional Chinese
Three threads, one per question type he named: 시술 보유 여부 / 가격 / 예약 가능 날짜.

This *is* the product to him and it will be read more closely than anything else. He
can judge in ten seconds whether it sounds like his clinic or like a robot.

- Prices labelled **예시 데이터 · Demo data** — inventing real prices is both a
  credibility risk and a 의료법 56/57 problem.
- Show the qualification working, not just the answer: the bot establishing travel
  dates and budget band before offering a slot.

### 2. His workflow, drawn — before / after
The five-step chain from the discovery meeting: DM → qualification → confirmation →
Korean employee → Google Calendar → CRM re-entry. Then the proposed flow.

The "you actually listened" asset. Operators are disproportionately moved by seeing
their own mess rendered accurately. Mark both pain points visually: the week-long
backlog at the front, the manual re-typing at the back.

### 3. The human-fallback moment
What happens when the bot *cannot* answer — handoff, staff notification, queue.

His unspoken fear is a bot embarrassing his clinic in front of patients. Showing the
bot knowing its own limits reassures more than showing it being clever. Address it
before he raises it.

### 4. 90-day plan, two tracks, drawn honestly
- **Track 1 — Instagram DM qualification.** Ours to deliver. Dated milestones.
- **Track 2 — Vegas CRM read/write.** Marked *dependent on API access*, with the
  customer-request step (see outreach log) shown on the timeline.

Doing double duty: it shows the plan and surfaces the Vegas dependency in writing
without an awkward speech, and it plants the request-to-Vegas as a scheduled step
rather than a favour.

**Do not draw Track 2 as a promise.** A good-looking document makes that trap worse —
he will screenshot it and remember it.

### 5. One line on pricing, on the page
> 저희는 건당 수수료를 받지 않습니다. 월 고정 이용료입니다.

Per `deal_risks.md` R2 this has not yet been said to Midam, and it is more urgent here
than usual because of the Taiwan broker arrangement (R1).

## Leave out

Company story, founder background, feature matrices, competitor comparisons, "AI" as a
headline, stock photography. He did not ask what we are.

## Close with an ask

Request **20–30 anonymised real DMs** to rebuild the mockups from his actual inquiries.

Turns a one-way send into an exchange, gets us the data to size the simple-vs-human
split that the whole 90-day scope depends on, commits him to a small action (which
predicts larger ones), and version two of those mockups in his patients' own words will
outperform anything we invent.
