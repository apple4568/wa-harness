# Busan — field notes

## Why Busan is not a detour

2025 foreign medical tourists in Busan: **75,879, up 151.5% YoY — first time ever
ranked #2 nationally.** Dermatology is **67%** of that, and derm visitors alone grew
**+301% YoY**. Taiwanese and Japanese patients are driving it into Haeundae, where
clinics are visibly building multilingual consultation and stay-length treatment
plans. The city has put **₩2.4B into a 2026 의료관광 활성화 기본계획**.

Implication: Busan clinics are mid-build on exactly the capability WA Harness sells,
the market is small enough that being early is still possible, and unlike Gangnam they
are not yet saturated with vendors. Seoul is 87.2% of volume; Busan is where the
*second derivative* is.

## Geography

- **서면 메디컬스트리트 (부산진구)** — ~160 cosmetic/medical clinics in one walkable
  district, one of three medical districts the city is developing. The highest clinic
  density per hour of walking anywhere in Busan.
- **해운대** — where the Taiwanese/Japanese derm growth is landing. Fewer clinics,
  better-fit clinics.

## The walk-in, reframed so it cannot fail

The current walk-in asks for the one thing the desk cannot give (a meeting with the
원장), so every visit returns zero. Change the ask to things the desk *can* answer and
every rejection still pays:

1. 실장님 성함이 어떻게 되세요? → a name
2. 외국인 문의는 주로 인스타 DM으로 들어오나요? 왓츠앱이나 라인도 쓰세요? → channel map
3. 명함 한 장만 실장님께 전달 부탁드려도 될까요? → a task they can complete

Success for the day = 20–30 names and a channel map, not a meeting. Leave in 45
seconds. Never say 유치.

## The formal procedure that actually exists

There is **no vendor-meeting intake process at 의원급**. 병원급 hospitals have a 기획팀
or 원무과 that accepts a formal 공문/제안서; a 의원 has no such function, no vendor
portal, no form. Stop looking for that door — it does not exist, which is exactly what
"she only meets people she wants to meet" means.

What does exist is membership in the bodies the 원장 already belongs to. You do not
request a meeting; you join the room she is already in.

## "She only meets people she wants to meet"

Read it as a spec, not a wall. Three things put someone on that list, in order of
reliability:

1. **소개** — an introduction from someone she already trusts: another 원장, her 장비
   영업사원, her 세무사, her 개원 컨설턴트, her 마케팅 대행사, a 학회 colleague. This is
   the dominant B2B mechanism in Korean healthcare, not a shortcut around it.
2. **Shared context** — 협의회, 학회, city programme. Peers meet peers.
3. **She needs what you have.** "They do outbound, not inbound" means she is actively
   hunting for foreign patients. Be visibly a source of that and the vector reverses.

Note also: the person who decides who she *wants* to meet is usually the 실장. That
sentence describes the 실장's job. Build the relationship with the filter rather than
trying to route around it.

## Line on mystery shopping

- **In person, posing as a patient: no.** It consumes a 상담실장's live time under a
  false identity, and it surfaces the moment you return as a vendor. That is a
  relationship you cannot repair.
- **Written enquiry to a public DM channel: fine.** Public inbox, asynchronous, costs
  them one reply, and it measures the exact thing you sell against. Use a reachable
  contact, answer honestly if asked whether you are a vendor.
