# 04 · Clinic Brochure / Leave-Behind — Format & Framework Research

Accessed 2026-09-16. Scope: what format a clinic-facing sales piece should take, and what content
framework most reliably converts a cold drop-off into an inbound enquiry from the 대표원장.

Method note: sources are English-language B2B sales-collateral literature plus Korean clinic-market
and clinic-sales sources reachable through a US-indexed search tool. Two directly relevant pages
(`stryvemarketing.com` sell-sheet blueprint, `mediking.net` on reading a clinic's decision-maker)
were blocked by the egress proxy and are summarised only from search snippets — flagged inline.
Facts carry an [n]; inferences are marked **가설 / hypothesis**.

---

## 1. The field finding, restated as a design constraint

Observed on door-to-door visits: clinics do not take inbound sales. The 대표원장 initiates — either
they hit an acute need and go looking for a source, or someone introduces the vendor.

That is not an objection to overcome; it is a specification. It means the piece we hand over is
**never read in the room where it is handed over**. Three consequences drive every decision below:

1. **It must survive the desk.** The person receiving it (데스크 / 상담실장) is not the buyer. The piece
   has to be legible and persuasive to someone who was not part of the conversation, because that
   person decides whether it reaches the 원장 or the recycling bin. Korean clinic-sales practice
   frames this explicitly: read the terrain of who is friendly and who is not, and make the
   recommendation happen *inside* the clinic — 원장 is persuaded by 경영적 이득 (cost, clinic image,
   patient satisfaction), 실무자 by 사용 편의 and 시간 절약 [7]. A single piece must therefore carry
   **both registers on different panels**, not average them into one.
2. **It must make its own case unaccompanied.** In B2B this is the standard test for a one-pager:
   it is the document the buyer forwards internally to get approval, and if it does not make that
   internal sale for them, it failed [1][2]. Here the "internal sale" is 실장 → 원장.
3. **It must manufacture the trigger, not wait for it.** "원장이 정말 필요해서 찾아 나선다" describes a
   buyer who has already recognised the problem. A brochure that describes a solution reaches only
   that small set. The piece has to *create* problem-recognition — which is a different content
   framework (§4), not a different design.

---

## 2. Format decision

### 2.1 What the literature says
- A one-pager is a single page, roughly **200–350 words of copy** plus visuals, data points, a CTA
  and contact details; PDF for hand-off and attachment [1][2].
- Tri-fold is the default for B2B capability statements and service overviews: six panels give a
  cover teaser, three content panels, and a back panel for contact; it fits a #10 envelope and a
  brochure rack, which is why it dominates lobbies and trade-show tables [8][9].
- Sell-sheet structure that recurs across sources: headline value proposition → buyer pain →
  solution → key benefits → social proof → clear CTA → contact. A two-column variant puts
  problem/solution in plain language on the left and the proof point, visual and CTA on the
  right [1][3].
- **80% of readers scan headlines and images before any body copy**; B2B buyers scan brochures
  rather than read them [3]. The back panel is the natural home for the primary CTA, differentiated
  by contrasting colour, larger type or a colour block [3].

### 2.2 Recommendation for Synaptix
**A two-piece system, not one brochure.**

| Piece | Format | Job | Who reads it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — The leave-behind** | A4 bi-fold (4 panels), or A4 duplex single sheet | Survives the desk; gets forwarded/handed to 원장; makes the case unaccompanied | 실장 first, 원장 second |
| **B — The self-diagnostic** | A4 single sheet, checklist/score format | Manufactures problem-recognition; converts a passive reader into someone who calls | 원장 (or 실장 filling it in and showing 원장) |

Why bi-fold over tri-fold: a tri-fold's six panels are built for a **catalogue of services** [8][9].
Synaptix has one proposition, not six, and the brand spec forbids claiming named integrations or
module lists it cannot yet support. Four panels forces the discipline the one-pager literature
recommends (200–350 words) while still giving a cover panel that works as a teaser when folded.
Keep the flat sheet option in reserve: a duplex A4 photographs and emails better, and the forwarded
version is the one the 원장 actually sees. **가설**: in a Korean clinic a folded piece reads as
brochure/광고 and a flat sheet reads as 자료 — the flat sheet may survive the desk better. Worth
printing both and tracking which comes back.

Why piece B matters most: the interactive self-assessment is reported as the best-converting B2B
lead-magnet format, beating downloadable PDFs because it converts on a personalised result rather
than a generic promise; once a prospect has seen their own number, a follow-up call becomes the
obvious next step rather than an interruption [6]. Diagnostic variants include maturity assessments
and **gap analysis — where the buyer falls short compared to peers** [6]. That is exactly the
mechanism that turns "원장이 스스로 찾아 나선다" from a barrier into the acquisition channel: the
checklist is how the 원장 discovers the need, on their own, in their own office, from our sheet.

## 3. Which persuasion framework fits — and which does not

| Framework | Fires when | Fit here |
|---|---|---|
| **AIDA** | Cold traffic, display ads, cold email; mirrors first encounter with something unknown [4] | Partial. Good for the cover panel only. Reported as no longer a one-size-fits-all spine [4] |
| **PAS** (Problem-Agitate-Solution) | Audience **already feeling** the pain; urgent/time-sensitive [4] | Wrong as the spine — our reader does not yet name the pain. Right at the micro level inside one panel |
| **BAB** (Before-After-Bridge) | Audience **wants change but needs a path**; BAB assumes motivation, PAS creates it [4] | Wrong as the spine, for the same reason. Strong for the product panel once the need is accepted |
| **StoryBrand** | Homepage heroes, about and sales pages; skip on pricing and reference docs [4] | Useful posture (clinic = hero, Synaptix = guide) but too narrative for a 300-word sheet |
| **Challenger / Commercial Teaching** | Buyer has **not recognised the problem**; leads with insight and reframes before proposing anything [5] | **Correct spine.** Its six steps — Warmer, Reframe, Rational Drowning, Emotional Impact, A New Way, Your Solution — put the solution last, after the buyer accepts that their current thinking produces a bad outcome [5] |

Supporting datum: in sales calls where the rep introduced a counter-intuitive reframe in the first
12 minutes, close rates were reported **41% higher** than calls that mostly confirmed the buyer's
assumptions [5]. Treat the exact figure as vendor-reported, but the direction is the design
principle: **open with something they did not already believe about their own clinic.**

How to build the reframe: the strongest ones take the shape *"the clinics that struggle most aren't
the ones with X, they're the ones with Y"* — found by looking across recent deals for what the
losers had in common that they did not see coming [5]. Synaptix has the raw material for this from
the ~26 Gangnam-area clinics already worked (see `01_existing_identity.md`) — the reframe must come
from what was actually observed there, not invented.

## 4. Recommended content flow (Piece A, panel by panel)

Challenger spine, PAS inside the pain panel, BAB inside the product panel. KO leads throughout
(brand spec §4: 합니다체 for sales, no superlatives, no exclamation marks).

**Panel 1 — Cover · Warmer + Reframe**
One counter-intuitive sentence about *their* clinic, verifiable by them without us. Not a product
claim, not a tagline. Logo small, bottom. Nothing else. This is the panel that decides whether the
piece is picked back up off the desk. Z-pattern applies to single-panel layouts: headline top-left,
supporting image top-right, body bottom-left, CTA bottom-right [10].

**Panel 2 — Rational Drowning · make the cost visible**
Their numbers, not ours. This is where the market evidence earns its place:
- 2025 신규개설 일반의 의원 10곳 중 약 8곳이 피부과; 2024년 7월까지 신규 개설 의원의 80%가 피부과 [11][12];
  2025년 신규 개설 피부과 243곳 중 약 65%가 피부질환 건강보험 청구 실적 전무 [11].
- 2025 일반의 신규개설 285곳 중 약 66%가 수도권, 강남구에서만 50곳 [11].
- 외국인 환자 200만 명 돌파, 피부과는 중국·대만 환자 중심으로 수요 증가 [13].
Read together these say: **the patient is harder to win and more expensive to win than last year,
and more of them do not speak Korean.** That is the commercial insight — the cost of a lost or
dropped enquiry has gone up, whether or not the clinic has noticed.

**Panel 3 — Emotional Impact + A New Way + Your Solution**
Concrete, at the desk, on a busy Saturday — the hand-off that gets dropped between 접수 → 상담 →
시술 → 결제 → 재방문. Then the *principle* (연결된 운영), then Synaptix as the thing built for it.
Map to the three pillars from the brand spec (연결된 운영 / 믿을 수 있는 정확성 / 명료한 속도), each with
its stated proof type. Use a real screen crop at working density, flat, no floating devices
(`02_competitors.md` §6.6).

**Panel 4 — Back · Proof, risk reversal, CTA, contact**
Primary CTA lives here, in a contrasting colour block [3]. See §5 and §6.

### The 실장 register
Panel 3 is the only place both registers coexist. Give the 실장 one explicit line in their own
terms — 시간 절약, 클릭 감소, 인수인계 — because they decide whether the piece travels [7]. Do not bury
it in the 원장's 경영 language.

## 5. Proof, when you have no customers to name

The brand spec forbids invented integrations, certifications, customer counts, results, uptime and
reliability guarantees, and the ~26 Gangnam clinics in the Drive sheets are **leads, not customers** —
they cannot be counted as adoption. Constraints on the honest alternatives:
- **Proprietary research as the credibility asset.** Early-stage vendors use their own research to
  become a credible source and to validate claims they make [14]. The competitor and buyer research
  already in this repo is publishable; the brochure can cite it, and it doubles as the reframe.
- **Stated process instead of stated outcomes.** The documented buyer concerns are stability
  (48.16% as the #1 EMR selection criterion, Medigate n=1,030), outage fear (48.4%), security
  (37.6%/38.5%) and customisation (30.3%) (`02_competitors.md`, sources 6–7 there). Answer each with
  a *procedure* the reader can inspect — 도입 절차, 데이터 처리 방침, 지원 체계, 백업 주기, 해지 시 데이터
  반출 — never a number we cannot evidence.
- **Risk reversal beats social proof at this stage.** "When you're tiny, show audacity; when you're
  bigger, show proof" [14]. The pilot offer *is* the proof panel.
- **Show, don't describe.** SaaS buyers want to see the product; offer a hands-on look in the first
  interaction [14]. A screen crop on the sheet plus an in-clinic demo as the CTA satisfies this.

## 6. The response mechanism

- **Specific beats generic.** "Call us today for a free 30-minute consultation" outperforms
  "Contact us"; a deadline ("book by [DATE]") consistently improves response [3].
- **Give two CTAs at different commitment levels**, not one: a low-friction one (the diagnostic
  sheet, a 15-minute call) and a high-intent one (30-minute in-clinic demo). The one-pager
  literature's CTA rule is specificity, not scarcity of options [1][3].
- **Named human, not a company.** For cold drop-offs the callback goes to the rep who left a
  specific note about why they were there, not a generic card [15]. Put a name, a face, a direct
  mobile and a KakaoTalk channel QR on the back panel — a 대표번호 alone reads as a vendor, not a
  person.
- **Date and name the copy by hand.** 가설: a handwritten clinic name + date on the cover marks it
  as delivered-in-person rather than 뿌리는 전단, which is the difference between the desk filing it
  and binning it. Cheap to test.

### The introduction path — build it into the piece
Introductions are one of the two stated ways these clinics buy, so it should be a designed feature,
not a hope:
- Hand over **two copies** and say one is for a 원장 they know. Costs nothing; makes the referral ask
  concrete instead of abstract.
- Put one line on the back panel that makes forwarding socially easy (an offer that extends to the
  introduced clinic, so the introducer is doing a favour rather than making a recommendation they
  have to defend).
- The pilot-slot framing ("이번 분기 도입 지원 N곳") gives the introducer a reason to act now. **Only
  use a number that is actually true.**

## 7. What must not go in

- No "AI" as a headline or primary selling point; no neural/brain/mesh imagery (brand spec §1).
- No 혁신 / 최고 / superlatives, no exclamation marks, no medical outcome claims (brand spec §4).
- No before/after imagery, no patient faces, no testimonials (`brand_spec.md` §7b; 의료법 제56조,
  제57조 restrict 의료광고 and require prior 심의 for certain media [16][17]).
- **Regulatory scope note:** 의료광고 is defined as 의료인등이 …소비자에게 의료행위·의료기관·의료인 정보를
  나타내거나 알리는 행위 [16]. A vendor-to-clinic B2B sheet is not, on its face, 소비자 대상 의료광고, and
  no explicit B2B carve-out was found in the sources reached. Two practical rules follow: (a) keep
  every claim about *our software*, never about *medical outcomes*; (b) if the sheet shows any
  patient-facing mock-up (알림톡 문안, 예약 화면), assume the clinic would have to clear it, and label
  demo content **"예시 데이터 · Demo data"** per the brand spec. Confirm with 보건복지부 or a 심의기구
  before printing anything that looks patient-facing.
- No invented uptime %, certifications, customer counts or results (brand spec, repeated guardrail).

## 8. Benchmarks, so expectations are calibrated

- Catalogues and brochures average about **3.2%** response in direct mail, exceeding 5% only with
  strong creative and precise targeting; postcards run 4–4.5% [10]. Door-to-door hand delivery is
  better targeted than mail, but **plan on single-digit percentages** — roughly 1 in 20 to 1 in 30
  clinics responding to a good piece.
- That maths says the brochure is a **multiplier on visit volume, not a substitute for it**, and
  that the diagnostic sheet (piece B) and the introduction mechanism (§6) are where the leverage is,
  because both raise the per-clinic conversion rather than the clinic count.

## 9. Open decisions before building

1. **The reframe sentence.** Must come from what was actually observed across the ~26 Gangnam
   clinics. This is the single highest-leverage line in the piece and cannot be researched — only
   recalled.
2. **Flat sheet vs bi-fold** — print both, track which comes back (§2.2).
3. **Which pilot terms are true and repeatable** — the risk-reversal panel is only as good as the
   offer behind it (§5).
4. **Whether the foreign-patient angle leads or supports.** It is the origin of the product and is
   backed by the strongest external number (200만 [13]), but the brand spec deliberately widened
   positioning away from the point tool. Leading with it narrows the addressable clinic list;
   supporting with it keeps the OS positioning intact.
5. **KO-only or KO/EN.** Bilingual signals foreign-patient readiness (`02_competitors.md` §6.11) but
   costs half the panel space at 300 words.

## 11. Limitations

- Two of the most on-point sources (Stryve's B2B sell-sheet blueprint; the mediking piece on reading
  a clinic's decision-maker) were **blocked by the egress proxy**; both are represented by search
  snippets only. The 원장/실장 message split in §1 and §4 rests on a snippet and should be confirmed
  against practitioners before it drives copy.
- No Korean-language research was found on **brochure or leave-behind design specifically for clinic
  sales**. Every format and flow recommendation above is imported from English-language B2B
  collateral literature; cultural fit in a Korean clinic lobby is **unvalidated 가설**.
- The 41% Challenger close-rate figure and the direct-mail response benchmarks are vendor-published
  and undated in the snippets; use them to choose a direction, not to forecast.
- No 피부과 폐업률 or patient-acquisition-cost statistic was found despite targeted search. The
  "harder and more expensive to win a patient" claim in §4 is an **inference** from opening density
  and the 65%-no-insurance-claims datum — it must be phrased as an observation about the market,
  not asserted as a measured cost.
- The search tool is US-indexed; Korean sources are under-represented relative to what a Naver or
  Korean-domain pass would surface.
